# Product Add-ons

The **Product Add-ons for Shop** module (`product-addons-for-shop`) lets a product offer other products alongside itself - screens with a bench desk, a cable tray, extra shelves with a cupboard. The shopper ticks what they want in a small panel under the Add to basket button, makes any remaining choices, and adds each add-on with its **own Add to cart button**. Add-ons never ride along on the main product's button; whichever order the two are bought in, the basket still gathers them into a tidy group.

It needs the Shop and Shop Variations modules. Product 3D Views and the Space Planner are optional - with them, the product picture can become the product *with* its add-ons.

## What the shopper sees

- **The add-ons panel** sits under Add to basket (place the `Shop: Product add-ons` block beneath `Shop: Add to basket` in the product layout - it renders nothing on products without add-ons). Each add-on is a row: tick it and its remaining choices appear, with a quantity field and an Add to cart button styled to match the main product's own. The row's little picture follows the choices: the exact variation once they resolve one, and before that the nearest variation to whatever is already settled - so an add-on whose colour is matched to the desk shows *that* colour while its fabric is still to pick, rather than whichever finish the listing photograph happens to be in. The listing's own picture is used only when nothing is settled at all.
- **Sizes sort themselves out.** An option set to *match* follows the main product silently - pick a 1400mm desk and the screens are 1400mm, no question asked. If a size has no matching add-on size, that add-on simply says it isn't available for that choice. The panel still states what it settled on ("Width: 1400mm - matches your width"), swatch and all, rather than deciding behind the shopper's back.
- **A value fixed by you is stated, not hidden.** An option set to *always one value* is not a choice, so it gets no buttons - but the panel names it ("Colour: White") and shows its swatch, so nobody is surprised by what turns up.
- **Colours follow, until overridden.** An option set to *follow, changeable* pre-selects from the main product (frame colour matching the desk's) and keeps following as the shopper changes their mind - until they change *it*, after which their choice sticks. A "Match my choice again" link puts it back. The option's name says where the pick came from: "Frame Colour - White (matching your choice)".
- **Recommendations, until overridden.** An option set to *pick a recommendation, changeable* pre-selects a value the admin chose and names it - "Frame Colour - White (recommended)" - rather than one derived from the main product. The shopper can pick something else, and a "Back to the recommendation" link restores it; picking the recommended value back by hand does the same thing, so the note returns and the link disappears. If the recommended value later disappears from the product it degrades to a plain choice.
- **Choices wait for the main product.** Where an add-on's option follows one of the main product's, its choices stay shut until that option is chosen - "Choose your Frame Colour above first" - rather than letting a shopper pick a colour that would be silently replaced a moment later.
- **The choices look like the product's own.** Each option's swatches and buttons sit after its name, and hovering a colour or picture pops the same enlarged look, with the value's name, that the main product page gives.
- **An add-on that comes one way** - a cable tray with no options at all - is simply added, no choices asked for.
- **Sold-out add-ons stand down.** An add-on with nothing left to sell disappears from the panel and the showcase alike, rather than sitting there tempting people into an order you cannot fill. Anything hanging off it (an accessory's own accessory) goes with it. Where only some of its variations have run dry, the add-on stays and those particular choices are struck through, marked *Out of stock* and unpickable - the rest of the range carries on as normal. Backorder and pre-order add-ons still sell past zero, exactly as they do on their own pages.
- **Quantities are suggested, not imposed.** A recommended-count add-on pre-fills the right number (3 screen sets for a 6 Person desk) and shows a gentle "We'd recommend…" note if changed. A free-count add-on (extra shelves) just starts at 1 and lets them pick.
- **Learn more** opens the add-on's full description - pictures, designed description, specification - in a popup, without leaving the page, with a Close button under the description.
- **Clicking an add-on's picture opens its pictures.** Both in the panel and on the showcase card, the picture is a button: it opens a popup with a large view and a thumbnail strip underneath, walkable with the arrow keys and closed with Escape. **The variation's own photographs come first** and the listing's general pictures follow, with a hairline in the strip where one group ends and the other begins - the same variation the row's thumbnail is showing, so the big view never disagrees with the picture that opened it. An add-on that comes exactly one way just shows the listing's pictures.
- **The showcase** is an extra tab in the product page's own tab strip (after Specification, named with your word - see Settings), with a card per add-on: Learn more, and an Add button that jumps back to the configurator with that add-on opened. Its pictures follow the panel's: choose a desk finish up top and the accessory cards further down the page change to match, rather than sitting there in whatever finish the catalogue photograph happened to be. On a page with no add-ons panel at all there is nothing to follow, so the cards keep the listing's own pictures.

## The basket, checkout and documents

Each add-on becomes its **own basket line** - priced, stocked, taxed and delivered exactly as if bought from its own page - grouped under the main product with an "Accessory for…" caption (once each; the caption is the label). Inside a group the basket draws no dividing line between the product and its accessories, so the set reads as one entry. The grouping follows through checkout, the order confirmation, order emails, the admin order view and quote documents (PDF included).

The group forms whichever way round the shopper buys: adding an add-on tags a main product already in the basket, and adding the main product afterwards joins add-ons already there. An add-on bought without its main product at all is simply a normal line, with a note saying what it was chosen for.

Removing the main product asks "Remove its accessories too?" - either answer, with a working Undo that restores the whole set. Quantities stay independently editable; if they drift from the recommendation, the basket line notes what the recommendation would be.

## Admin

- **Per product**: the *Add-ons* section on the product edit screen. Search for a product to link (loops are refused, as is a product with a *required* personalisation field - the panel cannot collect those). For each of the add-on's options choose: matches a main option / follows one but changeable / pick a recommendation but changeable / shopper chooses / always one value. Value translations are automatic (shared attribute, then matching name) with a per-value override table for exceptions. Set the quantity rule, the 3D context key and whether the space planner may place it on its own. Warnings appear for anything that would silently not work - a desk width with no matching screen width, a missing 3D file. **The order is yours**: each add-on shows its place in the list ("2 of 5") with up and down arrows, and that order is the one shoppers meet on the product page and in the showcase alike. The move saves on the spot, and nothing you were half way through typing is lost when you shuffle.
- **Shop → Product add-ons**: an overview of every link in the catalogue, and an **Attach rate** report - for each pairing, how many orders contained the main product, how many had the add-on bought with it, units and revenue, over a date range. Only add-ons genuinely bought *as* attachments count.
- **You still see the sold-out ones.** Signed in with shop access, the panel and the showcase show every add-on, with an *Out of stock* badge and a line saying shoppers cannot see it. Sold-out choices are struck through but still clickable, so you can configure and demonstrate one - the Add to cart button stays shut, so nobody can accidentally sell what is not there. Signed out, or as an ordinary shopper, none of that is in the page at all.
- **Settings** (Shop settings → Product add-ons): what add-ons are called sitewide - singular and plural (Deskwell says "Accessory"/"Accessories") - and where the showcase appears: the automatic tab, only where the showcase block is placed, or nowhere.

## 3D combined models (optional)

With Product 3D Views installed, a model file can be **tagged** with the add-on combination it shows. Upload a desk-with-screens file against the same variation as the plain desk and tag it `screens` (the little tag chip beside the model, in the 3D lists); tag quantity-specific files `shelves:1`, `shelves:2` and so on. When the shopper ticks the add-on, the viewer swaps to the tagged file and paints it live - fabric, frame colour, overrides and all. AR uses the same model.

The rule is **exact or base**: a combination without its own tagged file shows the plain product, never a guess. An add-on at a non-recommended quantity drops out of the preview (the panel says "3D preview shows the standard arrangement"). The admin section shows how many variations have files for each tag, so gaps are a choice rather than a surprise.

After uploading combined files, run **Detect + Save** in the fabric panel as usual - a file the config has never measured tiles its fabric wrong, exactly as with any other model.

## Space planner (optional)

The combined model follows the shopper into the planner. A desk bought with its screens stages as **one thing** - the desk drawn with the screens on it, from the tagged file - and the screens do not also turn up leaning against the wall as loose panels. The same holds whichever order the two went into the basket.

An add-on that contributes no combined-model tag stages the way it always did: its own placeable item where the link allows it to stand alone (a pedestal), list-only for a fit-inside part like a shelf, which still appears in the plan's parts list. Lines of one group stay tied together in the plan's paperwork.

Take an add-on back out on the basket page and the desk goes back to being a plain desk in the planner - the room follows the basket, not what the basket used to say. The **exact or base** rule still applies: a combination with no tagged file draws the plain product.

Putting a plan back into the basket restores each line as it was bought - grouping and personalisation included. Items placed from the planner's own catalogue stay plain products, as they always were.

## For developers

- Tables: `pad_links`, `pad_settings`. Config stores option **names** and value **slugs**, never ids, so sheet re-imports cannot break mappings.
- Seams used: `shop.cart-line-resolver` (+ prefetch, which now receives the whole cart's lines), `shop.product-editor-sections`, `shop.product-detail-tabs` (with the new `labelFor`), `shop.settings-sub-tabs`, shop's generic basket **groups** (`CartLineGroup`, `lib/cart-group.ts`), shop's client cart (`addToCart`/`setLineMeta` - each add-on row adds its own line), shop-variations' **purchase companions** (`window.__cactusPurchaseCompanions`, now used only to stamp the main line with its group when related add-on lines are already in the basket) and its variant-selection broadcast, and three window events of its own: `cactus-shop-model-context` (consumed by Product 3D Views), `cactus-shop-accessory-focus` and `cactus-shop-addon-images`.
- The space planner contract is the `modelContext` bag on a cart line (documented in the planner's `lib/client/cart-bridge.ts`; neither module imports the other). Each **add-on** line states its own piece: `{ stage, bundleOf, qtyPerMain, contextKey?, valueIds? }` - `contextKey` present means it is inside the main line's combined model, so `stage` is `'none'` and it never stages as loose furniture. The **main** line carries the merged summary `{ contexts, extraValueIds, bundleKey, contextsFrom: 'bundle' }`, stamped whichever way round the two were bought (the add-on's own button, or the purchase-companion on the main add). `contextsFrom: 'bundle'` is the important half: it tells the planner the list is a summary of lines it can see for itself, so it **re-derives** it at staging time and an add-on removed on the basket page stops being drawn. A bag without that marker keeps its stored list, which is all an older basket has.
- The picture popup (`components/public/AddonImageModal.tsx`) is deliberately separate from Learn more: pictures only, no iframe. Its images come from data the payload already carried - the row's `displayImages` (see below) followed by the listing's `selector.baseImages`, deduplicated by URL so a picture filed under both appears once, in the earlier (variation) position.
- `displayImages` on each resolved add-on is the single source for both the row thumbnail and the picture popup, so the two can never disagree: the resolved variant's `imageUrls` when the combination is complete, otherwise `settledVariantImages()` - the first **enabled** variation carrying pictures that answers to every value settled so far, aliases counted through shop-variations' own `variantAnswersTo`. Empty for an option-less (`plain`) add-on and when nothing is settled, where the listing picture is the honest answer. Note this is deliberately MORE eager than shop-variations' own product gallery, which waits for a complete combination: an add-on's colour is routinely locked by the link while a second option is still open, and the listing photograph is very often a different finish.
- The showcase is a separate island (it renders inside shop's tab strip) and holds none of that state, so it does not re-derive `displayImages` - the box **publishes** them on `cactus-shop-addon-images` (`lib/addon-images.ts`: `{parentProductId, images: Record<linkId, string[]>}`, snapshot at `window.__cactusAddonImages`, JSON-deduped like the other broadcasts) and the showcase repeats them. Deliberately one source rather than two: re-deriving would mean shipping the whole box payload into the tab as well, and two copies of the maths free to disagree. Published for every add-on including chain children, ticked or not; an add-on with nothing better than its listing picture is **absent from the bag** rather than present-and-empty, so a showcase with no box on the page (nothing heard) and an add-on with no variation pictures both fall back to the server-rendered listing picture instead of rendering nothing. The showcase's cards carry the listing's images as an **optional** `images` field on `ShowcaseCard`, so a payload cached before this shipped simply renders an unclickable picture as it always did.
- Stock (`lib/stock.ts`): `isAddonOutOfStock` = the `plain` row's own flag, or every **enabled** variation out of stock (a product with no enabled variations is the owner's doing, not the warehouse's, and is left alone). `buildBoxPayload` resolves shop's `canSeeStockLevels()` once as `staffView` and, for anyone else, prunes sold-out add-ons and their children out of the payload before it leaves the server - so the showcase (built from the same payload) can never disagree, and nothing about them reaches the browser. `isValueOutOfStock(selector, constraints, optionId, valueId)` answers the per-value question: constraints are every **locked** value (matched or fixed - settled by definition) plus picks made ABOVE the option in display order, the same directional rule shop-variations' own `isValueAvailable` uses, so a later pick can never grey out an earlier row. Staff are stopped at the button rather than the control (`disabled={!r.variant.inStock}`), exactly as shop-variations does it.
- Ordering: `pad_links.position` (already what every read sorts by). `POST /api/m/product-addons-for-shop/admin/products/<id>/links/reorder` takes the whole `orderedIds` list, refuses anything that is not exactly that product's links (409 - the editor reloads and says so), and renumbers in one transaction with each statement pinned to the product id as well as the link id. The editor moves the row optimistically and deliberately does **not** re-fetch afterwards: a re-fetch hands every link editor a fresh `link` object, which resets its unsaved drafts.
- The Learn more popup iframes shop's chrome-free `/shop/products/<slug>/details` view (noindex). It hides the site chrome by hiding everything in `body` except `main` - if core ever wraps `main`, the chrome reappears inside the popup and that selector is the line to revisit. Embedding it at all relies on core's security headers permitting **same-origin framing** (`frame-ancestors 'self'` / `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN`, core ≥ the release carrying this change) - with older headers the popup renders blank.
